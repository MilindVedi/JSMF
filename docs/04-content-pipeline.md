# Content Pipeline

> This document captures the guiding philosophy behind how JSMF's question bank content is produced. The pipeline itself — the software, tooling, and workflows that implement the process described below — will be designed and built as a separate effort from the main application (see [Roadmap](./06-roadmap.md)). The purpose of writing it down here, ahead of that effort, is so that the main application's data model — how a question is represented, what metadata it carries — stays compatible with this pipeline once it exists, rather than needing to be reworked to accommodate it later.

## Why This Exists

As explained in [Product Vision](./01-product-vision.md), JSMF's initial content is built on memory-based / recall-based previous year questions. Official exam bodies do not publish the original papers, so the entire category of products in this space — JSMF included — works from what candidates remember of the exams they sat. The question this pipeline exists to answer is: how do you turn a scattered set of imperfect, partial, sometimes conflicting human recollections into a reliable, well-tagged, trustworthy question bank, without simply reproducing someone else's commercial content? The stages below are the answer.

## The General Flow

At a high level, content moves through the pipeline as follows: multiple recall/memory-based sources feed into an AI extraction and matching stage, which produces candidate recalls annotated with confidence and evidence scores. A doctor then reviews the underlying medical concept identified from those recalls, and — this is the important step — independently authors the actual JSMF question, rather than adapting or paraphrasing any source text. The doctor-authored question then goes through AI-assisted quality checks, and only after the doctor gives final approval does it enter the JSMF question bank.

## Stage-by-Stage Description

- **Extraction.** Raw recall text is pulled from the various memory-based sources available to the pipeline. At this stage the material is unstructured and unverified — it is simply the raw input the rest of the pipeline works from.
- **Normalization.** Extracted recalls are cleaned and standardized — inconsistent formatting, obvious transcription noise, and structural irregularities are ironed out so that recalls from different sources can be compared on equal footing.
- **Matching / duplicate grouping.** The pipeline identifies when multiple independent sources appear to be recalling the same underlying exam question, grouping those recalls together. This is what allows the system to treat a question as more than a single, possibly-unreliable, individual's memory.
- **Confidence / evidence scoring.** Each group of matched recalls is scored based on how strongly its members agree with one another. This score exists to guide reviewer prioritization — helping doctors focus attention where the pipeline has the most and best-agreeing evidence — and explicitly not to auto-publish anything. No question reaches students without a human doctor's review and approval, regardless of how high its confidence score is.
- **Concept identification.** Because individual recalls are often imperfect or partial, the pipeline surfaces the underlying medical concept or topic that the grouped recalls appear to be testing, rather than presenting the raw recall text as if it were a finished question.
- **Doctor review of concept.** A licensed medical professional reviews the concept the pipeline has surfaced, using their own clinical and academic judgment to confirm (or correct) what is actually being tested.
- **Independent doctor authorship.** This is the core step in the entire pipeline: the doctor writes the actual JSMF question and its explanation from scratch, testing the confirmed concept, without paraphrasing or adapting any source material. The output is the doctor's own original work product, informed by — but not derived from — the recalls that led to it.
- **AI-assisted quality checks.** The newly authored question is run through automated checks that flag inconsistencies, missing explanations, formatting problems, and similar issues. This stage is a safety net that catches mechanical problems before a human spends review time on the question — it is not a replacement for doctor judgment and does not itself approve or reject content.
- **Doctor final approval.** The authoring doctor (or a designated reviewing doctor) gives final sign-off on the question.
- **Publication.** Only after final approval does the question enter the live JSMF question bank, available to students.

## The Core Content-Integrity Principle

This point is important enough to state plainly, in the same terms used in [Product Vision](./01-product-vision.md): **JSMF is not building a system whose purpose is to copy commercial question-bank questions and paraphrase them.** The pipeline described above exists to identify, from multiple independent human recollections, what medical concept was likely tested in a real exam — and a licensed medical professional then writes an original question testing that same concept. The resulting content is original work, inspired by identified exam patterns, not a reproduction or adaptation of any copyrighted material belonging to another question bank or publisher. This distinction is what allows JSMF to build a large, exam-relevant question bank while remaining on solid legal and ethical footing, and it should be treated as a non-negotiable constraint on how the pipeline is designed, not a talking point layered on afterward.

## Provenance and Evidence Metadata

Even though the detailed implementation of this pipeline is out of scope for now and will be designed as a separate effort, the system should eventually maintain internal provenance and evidence metadata for every question in the bank — for example, which recalls or sources contributed to identifying the underlying concept, the confidence level assigned to that grouping, which doctor reviewed and authored the question, and the date of approval. This metadata is primarily an internal accountability and quality-assurance record: it is what lets the team answer, for any question in the bank, "how do we know this is a reasonable representation of what was actually tested, and who is responsible for it." Keeping this in mind now, even before the pipeline itself is built, is why the main application's question data model (see [Architecture](./03-architecture.md) and the mock data model in [UI/UX Plan](./05-ui-ux-plan.md)) should leave room for this kind of metadata rather than treating a question as a bare stem-and-options record with no history.
